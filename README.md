# Model Scaling Degrades Small-Object Detection in PCB Inspection
### An Empirical Study with Knowledge Distillation

> **Submitted to SOMET 2026** — Rashadul Nafis Riyad, Radowan Ahmed Baized, Syed Abrar Rafid, S. M. Tuhin, Zatul Alwani Shaffiei, Siti Nur Khadijah Aishah Ibrahim  
> Department of Electronic Systems Engineering, Malaysia-Japan International Institute of Technology (MJIIT), Universiti Teknologi Malaysia (UTM) Kuala Lumpur

## Executive Summary

Printed circuit boards (PCBs) are at the heart of nearly every electronic device. Even microscopic defects — a missing hole, a hairline short circuit, or a tiny sliver of copper out of place — can make an entire product fail or become a safety hazard. Manual inspection is slow and error-prone; automated AI inspection is the modern solution.

**The common assumption** is that a larger, more powerful AI model will detect defects better. **This research shows that assumption is wrong for small defects.** Bigger models actually get *worse* at spotting micro-scale PCB defects, even while improving overall accuracy.

**Our solution:** Instead of deploying a large, slow model, we train a compact, fast model to *learn from* a large model — a technique called Knowledge Distillation (KD). The small student model inherits the teacher's precision while running at nearly 8× the speed. The final distilled model achieves higher accuracy than the compact model trained alone, at no extra cost at inference time.

---

## Problem Statement

### Why Small-Object Detection Matters

PCB defects such as mouse bites, missing holes, and spurs occupy only a few dozen pixels within a 640×640 input image — placing them firmly in the "small object" category by COCO standards (area < 32² pixels). A model that misses these micro-scale anomalies would pass defective boards as good, causing downstream failures in assembled electronics.

### The Model Scaling Paradox

Benchmarking five YOLOv8 model variants (n/s/m/l/x) on the HRIPCB dataset reveals a counterintuitive phenomenon:

| Model | mAP50-95 | AP_small | FPS | Params |
|-------|----------|----------|-----|--------|
| YOLOv8n | 0.643 | **0.587** | 463.5 | 3.6M |
| YOLOv8s | 0.696 | **0.591** | 209.0 | 11.1M |
| YOLOv8m | 0.730 | 0.563 | 98.0 | 25.8M |
| YOLOv8l | 0.747 | 0.552 | 56.0 | 43.6M |
| YOLOv8x | 0.761 | 0.556 | 33.0 | 68.1M |

**Key insight:** mAP50-95 improves monotonically with model size (0.643 → 0.761), but AP_small *degrades* as models grow larger. The compact YOLOv8n (0.587) outperforms YOLOv8m (0.563), YOLOv8l (0.552), and YOLOv8x (0.556) on the metric that matters most for PCB inspection.

A plausible explanation: large models pretrained on COCO (dominated by medium/large objects) develop coarser spatial features, while compact models are forced into finer spatial representations that benefit small-defect localisation.

### The Research Gap

Can we keep the compact model's small-object sensitivity while closing the 0.097 mAP50-95 gap with the larger teacher? This is the central question this work addresses through knowledge distillation.

## Objectives

1. Quantify the AP_small degradation phenomenon across YOLOv8 model variants on PCB inspection
2. Design a KD framework compatible with YOLOv8's anchor-free, multi-label BCE architecture
3. Systematically ablate distillation strategies (logit, feature, combined, box regression) to identify what works and why
4. Investigate P3-scale-weighted feature distillation to further improve small-object sensitivity
5. Characterise when KD is most beneficial (pretrained vs. scratch student initialisation)

## Methodology

### Knowledge Distillation for YOLOv8

Standard KD uses KL-divergence over softmax outputs. YOLOv8 uses per-class binary cross-entropy (BCE) — there is no shared softmax, making standard KD incompatible. We derive a sigmoid-based formulation:

**Soft targets** are generated by temperature-scaling the teacher's raw logits:

```
p_t = σ(z_t / T)
```

The student is trained to match these targets via BCE, with a T² correction to prevent gradient suppression (following Hinton et al., 2015):

```
L_KD = T² · BCE(σ(z_s/T), σ(z_t/T))
```

**Total training loss** combines three components:

```
L = α · L_det + β · L_KD + γ · L_feat
```

Default weights: α=0.7, β=0.2, γ=0.1, T=4.0

### Feature Distillation via ChannelAdapter

Student (YOLOv8n) and teacher (YOLOv8m) feature maps have different channel depths ([128, 256, 512] vs [192, 384, 576]). A lightweight ChannelAdapter — three independent bias-free 1×1 convolutions, one per FPN scale — projects student features into the teacher's channel space. Both maps are L2-normalised before computing MSE, preventing scales with larger magnitudes from dominating the loss.

The ChannelAdapter is **discarded at inference**, imposing zero deployment overhead.

### P3-Scale-Weighted Feature Distillation

An additional ablation assigns non-uniform weights to the P3, P4, P5 FPN scales in the feature KD loss. Since P3 is the finest-resolution scale most sensitive to small objects, upweighting it may further improve AP_small. Two configurations are tested:

- **P3-heavy**: [0.6, 0.3, 0.1] for P3/P4/P5
- **P3-dominant**: [0.7, 0.2, 0.1] for P3/P4/P5

## System Architecture

### Pipeline Overview

```
Input Image Batch
       │
       ├──────────────────────────────────┐
       │                                  │
       ▼                                  ▼
┌─────────────┐                  ┌─────────────────┐
│  YOLOv8m    │ ← FROZEN         │   YOLOv8n       │ ← TRAINABLE
│  Teacher    │   (no gradients) │   Student       │
│  (25.8M)    │                  │   (3.6M)        │
└─────────────┘                  └─────────────────┘
       │                                  │
       ├── Raw logits (z_t)               ├── Raw logits (z_s)
       │                                  │
       ├── P3 features [192ch]            ├── P3 features [128ch] ──► ChannelAdapter ──►
       ├── P4 features [384ch]            ├── P4 features [256ch] ──► ChannelAdapter ──►
       └── P5 features [576ch]            └── P5 features [512ch] ──► ChannelAdapter ──►
                │                                  │
                └──────────┬───────────────────────┘
                           │
                    ┌──────▼──────┐
                    │  KD Losses  │
                    │             │
                    │ L_KD  (β)   │ ← Sigmoid BCE between logits
                    │ L_feat (γ)  │ ← L2-normalised MSE on features
                    │ L_det  (α)  │ ← Standard YOLOv8 detection loss
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           │
                    ┌──────▼──────┐
                    │  Total Loss │
                    │ L = α·L_det │
                    │   + β·L_KD  │
                    │   + γ·L_feat│
                    └──────┬──────┘
                           │ Backprop (student only)
                           ▼
                   Student weights updated

At inference: teacher + ChannelAdapter discarded
              only YOLOv8n student is deployed
```

### Implementation Details

- **Custom `KDTrainer`** extends Ultralytics' `DetectionTrainer`, injecting KD loss terms without modifying model architecture or inference pipeline
- **Forward hooks** capture P3/P4/P5 neck outputs from both models without architectural changes
- **Teacher** kept in `train()` mode (required for logit access) with BatchNorm layers forced to `eval()` mode; all passes wrapped in `torch.no_grad()`
- **Training**: 200 epochs, batch 16, SGD + cosine LR, seed 42, NVIDIA RTX 5000 GPU

### Per-Class Breakdown (Best Model)

Combined KD + P3-heavy, evaluated on the held-out test set (mAP50 = 0.9930, mAP50-95 = 0.6616), yields per-class mAP50-95 of 0.662 (missing hole), 0.655 (mouse bite), 0.693 (open circuit), 0.657 (short), 0.651 (spur), and 0.651 (spurious copper). Validation-set performance (mAP50 = 0.9903, mAP50-95 = 0.6527) is closely consistent with the test set, confirming the model generalises well to unseen data.

### Interpretation of Results

**What works:**
- Feature KD delivers the largest single-component AP_small improvement (+0.0085), directly aligning the spatial representations most critical for small defects
- Combined KD improves both metrics over every single-component variant — logit distillation improves class-level discrimination while feature distillation improves localisation
- Per-class analysis shows mouse bite benefits most from combined KD (+0.0198 mAP50-95), consistent with feature distillation's localisation-sensitivity for the smallest defect types
- P3-heavy weighting (0.6/0.3/0.1) achieves the highest AP_small of any configuration (0.6174) and the highest mAP50-95 (0.6616, test set), with notably tighter boxes (AP75 = 0.7412) — this establishes Combined KD + P3-heavy as the overall best configuration

**What does not work:**
- Box regression KD provides negligible gain (+0.0002 mAP50-95) — teacher and student converge to similar DFL representations, leaving no exploitable dark knowledge
- Excessive logit weight (β=0.3) hurts AP_small (-0.0035 below baseline)
- P3-dominant (0.7/0.2/0.1) reverses P3-heavy's gains — starving P4 of distillation signal is counterproductive

**Non-monotonic P3 response:**
Increasing P3 weight from 0.6 to 0.7 reduces AP_small from 0.6174 to 0.6113, below even equal-weight combined KD. This confirms that P4 features remain essential alongside P3, and that balanced multi-scale alignment is critical.

**Why gains are modest:**
Four structural factors cap KD improvements: (1) mAP50 saturation at ~0.992 leaves gains almost entirely in bounding-box tightness; (2) COCO pretraining acts as implicit pre-distillation, saturating the student before HRIPCB fine-tuning; (3) the teacher-student gap is localisation-driven, which logit KD cannot directly address; and (4) HRIPCB's synthetic, homogeneous defects allow the pretrained student to saturate without teacher guidance.

**KD is most valuable from scratch:**
KD improves scratch students by +1.8% relative (vs. +1.4% for pretrained), confirming that teacher guidance is most beneficial when the student lacks prior representations. In industrial settings where large-scale pretraining is unavailable for the target sensor modality, teacher-student KD provides substantially more benefit.

**Variance across seeds:**
To confirm gains exceed normal training noise, the student baseline and Combined KD + P3-heavy were each trained at three seeds (42, 43, 44). Across seeds, the baseline achieves mAP50-95 = 0.6497 ± 0.0012 and AP_small = 0.6072 ± 0.0012, while the proposed model achieves mAP50-95 = 0.6594 ± 0.0028 and AP_small = 0.6175 ± 0.0029. The improvement (≈0.0097 mAP50-95, ≈0.0103 AP_small) is roughly 3–4× larger than the standard deviation observed for either model, indicating the gain exceeds run-to-run variability.

### Final Model Recommendation

| Recommended Model | mAP50-95 | AP_small | FPS |
|------------------|----------|----------|-----|
| Combined KD + P3-heavy (α=0.7, β=0.2, γ=0.1, T=4, feat. wts 0.6/0.3/0.1) | 0.6616 (test) | 0.6174 | 288.1 |

## Impact and Significance

### Practical Applications

- **Edge deployment**: The distilled YOLOv8n student (3.6M parameters, 280 FPS) is a strong candidate for real-time on-device PCB inspection on edge hardware such as the NVIDIA Jetson Nano — no server-side inference required
- **Cost efficiency**: Nearly 8× faster than the teacher (37 FPS) at roughly half the accuracy gap
- **Zero inference overhead**: The ChannelAdapter used during training is discarded at deployment; the deployed model is a standard YOLOv8n with no extra components

### Research Contributions

1. **First systematic documentation** of the AP_small degradation phenomenon when scaling YOLOv8 on PCB inspection — larger is not always better
2. **Sigmoid-based KD formulation** compatible with YOLOv8's multi-label BCE architecture, where standard KL-divergence KD fails
3. **Comprehensive ablation** of distillation strategies (logit, feature, combined, box regression, temperature, initialisation) clarifying which techniques work and why
4. **P3-scale-weighted feature distillation** with a non-monotonic response revealing the importance of balanced multi-scale alignment
5. **Practical guidance**: KD is most beneficial when the student is trained from scratch — quantified evidence for practitioners deciding when to invest in distillation

---

## Limitations

- **Synthetic-to-real generalisation**: HRIPCB is synthesised from 10 PCB board layouts. A preliminary cross-dataset check on real-world PCB images showed a noticeable accuracy drop, consistent with the expected synthetic-to-real domain gap
- **Fixed teacher-student pair**: Only one teacher-student pair (YOLOv8m → YOLOv8n) is evaluated
- **Modest absolute gains**: KD improvements are consistent but small in magnitude (~0.009-0.010 mAP50-95), reflecting fundamental dataset saturation rather than a limitation of the KD framework

---

## Future Work

- **Full cross-dataset study**: Extend the preliminary real-world check into a complete cross-dataset evaluation with domain adaptation
- **Attention-guided distillation**: Focus knowledge transfer on foreground defect regions rather than uniform spatial alignment
- **Edge hardware evaluation**: Benchmark the distilled YOLOv8n on NVIDIA Jetson Nano for real-time embedded PCB inspection

## How to Reproduce

### 1. Environment Setup

```bash
git clone <repository-url>
cd pcb_kd_project

# Create and activate virtual environment
python -m venv venv
source venv/bin/activate  # Linux/macOS

# Install dependencies
pip install ultralytics pycocotools
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

### 2. Dataset

Download the HRIPCB dataset and place it at:

```
datasets/HRIPCB/
├── train/
│   ├── images/
│   └── labels/
├── val/
│   ├── images/
│   └── labels/
└── test/
    ├── images/
    └── labels/
```

The dataset YAML at `data/pcb_defect.yaml` is pre-configured for this layout.

### 3. Train the Teacher (YOLOv8m)

```bash
yolo detect train \
  model=yolov8m.pt \
  data=data/pcb_defect.yaml \
  epochs=100 imgsz=640 batch=8 \
  project=runs/experiments/teacher \
  name=yolov8m
```

### 4. Run KD Ablation Experiments

Open `models/kd/ablation_kd.py` and set the `RUN` variable to the experiment you want:

```python
RUN = "kd_combined"   # or: kd_logit_only, kd_feat_only, kd_box,
                      #     kd_combined_p3_heavy, kd_combined_p3_dominant,
                      #     scratch_baseline, scratch_kd_combined
```

Then run:

```bash
python models/kd/ablation_kd.py
```

Weights are saved to `runs/experiments/kd/<RUN>/weights/best.pt`.

### 5. Validate a Trained Model

Update `WEIGHTS` and `EXPERIMENT_NAME` in `models/kd/validate_kd.py`, then:

```bash
python models/kd/validate_kd.py
```

Results are saved to `runs/experiments/kd_val/<EXPERIMENT_NAME>/val_results.txt`.

### 6. Compute AP_small (COCO Eval)

First, generate the COCO annotation files:

```bash
python scripts/yolo_to_coco.py
```

Then run AP_small evaluation across all experiments:

```bash
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="" python scripts/compute_ap_small.py
```

Or for a specific experiment:

```bash
CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="" python scripts/compute_ap_small.py kd_combined
```

Results are saved to `runs/experiments/ap_small_results.csv`.

## Technical Deep Dive

### Why Standard KD Does Not Work for YOLOv8

Standard KD (Hinton et al., 2015) uses KL-divergence: `KL(softmax(z_t/T) || softmax(z_s/T))`. This requires a shared probability simplex over all classes via softmax. YOLOv8 uses independent per-class BCE — each class head outputs a logit treated as a binary probability, not a component of a multiclass distribution. Applying softmax would mix unrelated class logits.

Our solution applies temperature scaling and sigmoid independently per class, then computes BCE:

```
L_KD = T² · (1/N) · Σᵢ BCE(σ(zˢᵢ/T), σ(zᵗᵢ/T))
```

The T² factor restores gradient magnitude suppressed by the 1/T² term implicit in temperature scaling, following the derivation in Hinton et al. (2015) section 2.

### ChannelAdapter Design Choices

The adapter uses `Conv2d(C_s, C_t, kernel_size=1, bias=False)` — no BatchNorm, no ReLU:
- **No BN**: Avoids batch statistics during training that would interfere with feature alignment
- **No ReLU**: Preserves negative channel activations, which carry meaningful spatial information in L2-normalised feature space
- **1×1 conv**: Minimal parameter overhead; projects channel depth without mixing spatial information

### L2 Normalisation in Feature Loss

Without normalisation, deeper FPN levels (P5) have larger absolute feature magnitudes than P3, which would cause P5 to dominate the MSE loss regardless of scale weights. L2 normalisation along the channel dimension brings all scales to unit norm before computing MSE, making per-scale weighting meaningful and interpretable.

### P3-Scale-Weighted Loss Implementation

```python
# In kd_losses.py — feature_kd_loss()
if scale_weights is not None:
    w = torch.tensor(scale_weights, device=device, dtype=dtype)
    w = w / w.sum()   # normalise to sum to 1
else:
    w = torch.full((num_scales,), 1.0/num_scales, ...)

for i, (s_adapted, t_feat) in enumerate(zip(student_feats_adapted, teacher_feats)):
    s_norm = F.normalize(s_adapted, p=2, dim=1)
    t_norm = F.normalize(t_feat,    p=2, dim=1)
    scale_loss = F.mse_loss(s_norm, t_norm, reduction='none').sum(dim=1).mean()
    total_loss = total_loss + w[i] * scale_loss
```

Internal normalisation of `scale_weights` means the absolute values passed to the trainer do not need to sum to 1 — only their ratios matter.
